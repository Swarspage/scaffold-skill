# Visual direction vocabulary

Use a named direction as a decision framework, not a styling preset.

| Direction | Useful traits | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brutalism / neo-brutalism | direct geometry, assertive scale, exposed structure, deliberate contrast | arbitrary ugliness or inaccessible text |
| Swiss / International Typographic Style | grid discipline, asymmetry, precise hierarchy, sans typography | flattening the 3D object into a poster |
| Industrial minimalism | practical materials, controlled restraint, functional detail | turning every surface into dark chrome |
| Luxury editorial | generous negative space, crafted type contrast, calm pacing | unreadable hairline type or empty prestige claims |
| Neo-futurism / high-tech industrial | engineered light, technical geometry, confident spatial depth | blue-purple glow and generic HUD clutter |
| Organic futurism / biophilic | flowing forms, diffuse light, tactile materials, environmental depth | pretending primitive geometry is natural anatomy |
| Japanese minimalism | reduction, imperfect material nuance, interval and quiet | decorative clichés or a bare page with no hierarchy |
| Maximalism / postmodernism | intentional collision, layered color/type, playful rhythm | unranked noise and simultaneous animation |
| Retro-futurism | period-specific optimism and material cues, not nostalgia wallpaper | mixing eras without a reason |
| Cinematic product advertising | focal lighting, controlled crops, paced reveals, sparse copy | making the canvas a video with unusable controls |

For every selected direction, decide: display/body/mono roles; dominant/supporting/accent colors; shape and depth language; materials and light; DOM-versus-3D layering; and motion restraint. See `typography.md`, `color.md`, and `materials-and-lighting.md`.
